# CONTENT PLAN — Thai public-domain stories for ~20-minute episodes

Every entry below was checked against Thai Wikipedia or Wikisource. Death dates
are the legal test: Thai copyright lasts the author's life **plus 50 years**
(Copyright Act B.E. 2537, section 19), so a work is free once its author died
more than 50 years ago.

## Why not translate foreign stories

Episode 1 used O. Henry's *The Gift of the Magi*. The plot turns on one dollar
eighty-seven cents, and that number means nothing to a Thai listener — the
sacrifice only reads if you know what the money buys. Thai stories, or Thai
adaptations, avoid the problem entirely. Where a story must mention money,
convert it to something concrete (a bowl of noodles, a day's wage).

## What to avoid, and why

* **Works by authors who died after 1975** — still in copyright. This includes
  most modern Thai novels and short stories.
* **Translations** — a translation is its own copyrighted work even when the
  original is free.
* **Modern editions with annotations** — the annotations and the editorial
  arrangement carry their own rights. Use a plain text.
* **Anything from a fan site with no stated source.**

## Slate, in priority order

### ep02 — มัทนะพาธา (the rose that became a woman)

**Why first.** A love tragedy with one male and one female lead, so requirement 3
(male and female voices in dialogue) is exercised properly. Strong visual
imagery for poster art: a rose garden, a royal pavilion, a pyre of flowers.
It also holds the most quoted romantic verse in Thai.

The story: a celestial being is cursed to be born as a rose. A young man,
มาณพ, loves the rose; a god grants it human form as มัทนา. Their love cannot
survive the difference in their natures, and it ends in loss.

**Angle for the script.** Frame it as a story about loving something that cannot
stay. The tragedy is not betrayal, it is category — she is a flower, he is a man.

### ep03 — ข้างหลังภาพ (the portrait behind the frame)

**Why.** The most relatable to a modern listener: a love across an age gap and a
class gap, told with restraint. The frame device — the truth is in the portrait,
not the words — makes a good visual spine for poster cards.

**Angle.** Quiet, wistful. This is the episode to use the female voice as the
narrator for a stretch, because the emotional centre is hers.

### ep04 — ขุนช้างขุนแผน, a single episode

**Why with care.** The epic is enormous — adapting all of it is impossible and
would flatten it. Pick one ตอน with a self-contained arc, most likely
ขุนช้างถวายฎีกา or the early ขุนแผน–วันทอง courtship. State plainly on the card
that it is one episode of a larger epic.

### ep05 — ไกรทอง

**Why.** Folk material with a strong hook — a man, a crocodile spirit, and a
contest of love and magic. Faster and more exciting than the others, useful for
varying the channel's mood.

## Structure that works for ~20 minutes

| Part | Share | Content |
|---|---|---|
| เปิด | 0:00–1:30 | a scene from the middle of the story, to pose the question |
| ปูพื้น | 1:30–5:00 | who these people are, what they want |
| พัฒนา | 5:00–13:00 | the promise, the obstacle, the attempt |
| จุดพลิก | 13:00–17:00 | the loss or the reversal |
| ขมวด | 17:00–20:00 | what it meant, and a line the listener keeps |

Roughly 8,000–10,000 Thai characters, 250–300 lines, 14–18 poster cards.

## Sourcing images for each episode

Query Openverse for concrete nouns from the scene list, not abstractions:
`rose garden`, `thai temple pavilion dusk`, `candlelit bedroom`, `river boat
night`, `golden buddha statue`. Two images per query, then pick the ones that
match the grade (dark, saturation 0.29–0.60).
